A vulnerability disclosure program a small team can operate

A policy is a promise about how a team receives and handles reports. Keep that promise narrower than your aspirations: publish only channels you monitor, assign a real owner, and rehearse the path before inviting reports.

Short answer: start with an explicitly scoped policy, a monitored private intake route, one named accountable owner plus backup, a repeatable triage and reporter-update process, and a rehearsal using fictional data. Publish only commitments your team can keep.

Prepared by Beacon Community. Verify that your organization can meet any commitments it makes; this is not legal advice.

Who owns a vulnerability disclosure program?

Name a primary operator and a backup. Define who acknowledges reports, assesses impact, approves fixes, speaks with the reporter, and authorizes disclosure. If these roles are one person, say so internally and arrange backup coverage. A shared inbox without a responsible human is not a response process.

What should a vulnerability disclosure policy promise?

Describe in-scope products and domains, safe-harbor boundaries, testing rules, information to include, how to report, realistic response commitments, and how coordinated disclosure will be discussed. Avoid promising response or remediation times the team cannot meet. Do not invite testing of third-party infrastructure unless you have authority to do so. Start from the editable policy starter and replace every bracket before use.

How should a small team receive reports?

Provide a private reporting method that does not require a researcher to create an account. Test it from an ordinary external account and device. Confirm delivery, access control, attachment handling, retention, spam handling, and the human escalation path. Publish a backup route only if it is also monitored. Keep credentials out of the policy.

What does a workable triage process look like?

  1. Record receipt time, affected product/version, reproducible steps, impact claimed, and a safe way to reply.
  2. Acknowledge receipt without implying validity or severity.
  3. Assign one accountable owner and preserve original evidence with restricted access.
  4. Reproduce safely; assess affected versions, impact, exploit evidence, and any external reporting duties.
  5. Agree on status updates and disclosure coordination with the reporter.
  6. Record decisions, remediation, release, and closure rationale; review what should change in the process.

What can workflow software—and Beacon—not do?

A case tracker can preserve assignments, conversations, and an audit trail; it cannot staff an inbox, determine legal scope, assess exploitability, make product decisions, or file a regulatory report on the manufacturer's behalf. Beacon is self-hosted workflow software, not a managed response service or compliance guarantee. A team adopting it still owns monitoring, access, backups, response coverage, and disclosure decisions.

How can we rehearse safely? A synthetic Beacon walkthrough

Use a disposable local instance and fictional details such as “Northstar Sensor 2.4” and “sample input validation issue”—never a real system or finding. Submit the fictional report from the public form, save its one-time recovery token, and confirm the receipt page. Sign in with the locally seeded team account, assign the case, add an internal note, then send a separate reporter-visible reply. Reopen the reporter view with the token to verify that staff-only notes remain private. Finally inspect the history and create a selective export; confirm it contains only intended information. This exercises a tool path, not the team's real staffing, external alert delivery, legal decisions, or production controls. Follow the reproducible synthetic evaluation walkthrough.

What should be checked before launch?

  • Submit a clearly synthetic report from outside the staff account.
  • Confirm the reporter can return using the intended recovery mechanism.
  • Have a second staff member review permissions, notes, replies, exports, and audit history.
  • Exercise backup/restore and the escalation route; document who noticed and who acted.
  • Remove test data and verify the public page contains no secrets or real report details.
